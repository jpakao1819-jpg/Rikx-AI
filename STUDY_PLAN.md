# Rikx-AI: 12-week forex learning program

This curriculum builds foundational knowledge and a disciplined research process. It is not a recommendation to trade and does not promise that study will produce profitable predictions. Plan for about 3–5 hours per week; adapt the pace as needed.

## Month 1 — Foundations (Weeks 1–4)

### Week 1: Forex market basics
Study base/quote currencies, pairs, pips, lots, leverage, and how spot forex differs from exchange-traded currency products. Practice explaining a sample quote and calculating hypothetical pip value and notional size.

### Week 2: Market structure and execution
Study participants, sessions, liquidity, bid/ask spreads, orders, and slippage. Compare timestamped demo quotes at different times; do not place trades.

### Week 3: Leverage and risk
Study margin, position size, gaps, counterparty risk, and why stop orders do not guarantee an exit price. Calculate hypothetical loss scenarios and write a risk checklist.

### Week 4: Economic fundamentals
Study inflation, employment, growth, interest rates, and central-bank communications using primary sources. Summarize an official policy statement and relevant indicators; distinguish actual data, expectations, and revisions.

## Month 2 — Analysis and risk (Weeks 5–8)

### Week 5: Chart reading
Learn candlesticks, timeframes, trends, ranges, support/resistance, and indicator limitations. Annotate historical charts using only information available at the time.

### Week 6: Testable hypotheses
Define a hypothetical market, timeframe, entry, invalidation, exit, and no-trade conditions. Write clear rules before reviewing outcomes; do not treat patterns as guarantees.

### Week 7: Position sizing and guardrails
Study potential loss, drawdown, correlated exposure, and daily stop limits. Create a paper-only risk plan; risk controls cannot eliminate market risk.

### Week 8: Psychology and process
Study overconfidence, loss aversion, confirmation bias, and revenge trading. Make a pre-analysis checklist and journal uncertainty as well as opinions.

## Month 3 — Practice and review (Weeks 9–12)

### Week 9: Backtesting honestly
Use chronological training and test periods; avoid look-ahead bias. Include spreads, fees, slippage, missing data, and varied market conditions. Write a test protocol before running it.

### Week 10: Evaluate evidence
Review after-cost expectancy, drawdown, sample size, and results across regimes—not only win rate. Check probability calibration and compare to simple baselines.

### Week 11: Paper-trading simulation
Practice only with simulated funds or a spreadsheet and account for hypothetical costs. Record setups before outcomes and document any rule changes. A short simulation does not establish a reliable edge.

### Week 12: Review and next steps
Review data quality, rule-following, mistakes, uncertainty, and journal entries. Write a final report with sources, methods, costs, limitations, and what evidence would change your mind. Do not interpret a small backtest or simulation as proof of future profitability.

## Suggested public learning sources

- [CFTC — Learn & Protect](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect)
- [NFA — Investor Resources](https://www.nfa.futures.org/investors/investor-resources.html)
- [BIS — Triennial Central Bank Survey](https://www.bis.org/statistics/rpfx22.htm)
- [Federal Reserve — Monetary Policy](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy.htm)
- [CME Group — Education](https://www.cmegroup.com/education.html)

Use primary sources where possible, confirm current information, and assess jurisdictional relevance. These materials are references, not endorsements of a strategy.

## Safety boundaries

This program is educational, not financial advice. It does not execute trades or connect a brokerage account. Forex trading carries substantial risk and losses can exceed expectations, especially with leverage. There are no guaranteed predictions or profits.
